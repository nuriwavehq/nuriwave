01 • Academic fit

Start with the program.

Think department first, then institution, then location and lifestyle.

Choose your goal

Decide whether you are seeking undergraduate study, a master's, a PhD, exchange study or Korean-language training. Your goal determines which institutions and application routes deserve your attention.

Compare the department

Look at curriculum, course language, laboratories, research areas, faculty and the actual modules you expect to study. A university-wide reputation cannot tell you how well a particular department fits your goals.

Check the requirements

International admission requirements can vary between universities and departments. Check accepted language tests, academic thresholds, portfolios, interviews and the exact document format for every institution.

Build your timeline

Work backward from the official deadline and leave buffer time for language tests, recommendations, translations, authentication and portal uploads. A calendar prevents avoidable last-minute problems.

Understand GKS properly.

GKS is the Korean government's scholarship program for international students. The 2026 graduate program plans to invite 2,000 students; the undergraduate program has its own quota and structure. Track, degree level, university eligibility, deadlines and selection details differ by program and must be checked against the current official notice.

01 • What GKS is

Read the structure, not just the benefits.

GKS combines scholarship support with a formal selection system administered through NIIED and the relevant embassy or designated university. The official annual guideline controls the details.

What it is

A government-invited scholarship degree program designed to bring international students to Korea and strengthen educational and international networks. Study in Korea describes degree routes for undergraduate and graduate students as well as specialized programs.

Important: “GKS” does not mean every applicant follows the same process. Undergraduate and graduate programs use different annual guidelines.

What support can include

Study in Korea lists scholarship benefits including airfare, Korean-language training, tuition and study allowances for the GKS degree programs. The exact amounts, conditions and duration depend on the current program guideline.

Do not use old figures: benefit tables can change by year and program.

Who manages it

The National Institute for International Education (NIIED) is the central organization shown on the official Study in Korea GKS notices. The first screening stage is handled by the relevant embassy or designated university, depending on the track.

Where to submit

For degree programs, the official GKS information states that applicants submit either through the Korean Embassy in their country of citizenship or directly to a designated Korean university, depending on the track.

Graduate GKS includes broad fields across humanities, social sciences, natural sciences and related disciplines.

The current program page includes R&D, Global Network and other specialized routes. R&D is for science/engineering-related fields; Global Network is a master's-level route conducted mainly in English without Korean-language training.

03 • Strategy

What should an applicant actually research?

This is where NuriWave can be more useful than a generic scholarship post.

Track fit

Do not choose Embassy or University Track because one “sounds easier.” Start from your country route, program category, eligible universities and the current annual instructions.

University fit

Check the department, degree language, research or curriculum, scholarship conditions, location and international support. A famous name is not automatically the best personal fit.

Document fit

Read the annual document list line by line. Some documents may have specific translation, authentication, format or issue-date requirements.

Writing fit

Your personal statement and study plan should explain a coherent academic direction, why Korea and the chosen program fit it, and what you intend to do with the opportunity.

Official source: Study in Korea GKS notices. Current notices and attached university information are the final reference for the application year.

02 • Research each university properly

A university profile should answer these questions.

Don't stop at a name and logo.

Academic fit

What department would you actually enter? What is the curriculum, research environment, teaching language, faculty profile and laboratory or project ecosystem?

NuriWave principle: compare the department first, then compare the institution.

Admissions

What is the current application route, deadline, eligibility, document format, language requirement, interview policy and application fee? These belong to the university's current admissions page.

Never copy an old admissions date from a social post.

GKS relationship

What GKS category and track is the university participating in? Is your exact department listed? What Type A/Type B or specialized-program rule applies?

The annual GKS University Information file is the key verification document.

Living environment

Where is the campus? What is the surrounding neighborhood like? How long is the commute? What are dormitory options, food options and access to transport?

City can matter nearly as much as university name for everyday life.

01 • City stories

One country, many rhythms.

These descriptions are orientation guides, not live cost sheets.

Seoul

Seoul is Korea's capital and a major concentration of universities, technology, culture and public transport. The variety is enormous, but popular housing areas can be expensive and the pace can be fast. Students can find clubs, events, international communities and very different neighborhoods within the same metropolitan area.

Busan

Busan combines metropolitan services with a strong coastal identity. Beaches, mountains, port districts, markets and film culture shape daily life. It can appeal to students who want a major city while living close to the sea and experiencing a different rhythm from Seoul.

Jeju

Jeju is a volcanic island where nature is part of everyday identity. Beaches, cliffs, forests and regional traditions make it different from mainland metropolitan life. Students should consider program availability and practical travel requirements as well as scenery.

Daejeon

Daejeon is closely associated with science, research and technology, with universities and research institutions that can be attractive for STEM-focused students. The city can feel calmer than Seoul while remaining well connected.

Daegu

Daegu is a large inland city with universities, food culture, fashion and textile heritage, and nearby mountains. It offers a substantial urban environment while feeling different from the capital.

Gyeongju

Gyeongju is especially important for understanding Korean history, with extensive heritage connected with the Silla period. It offers a very different rhythm from a modern megacity and shows another side of Korea.

01 • Culture

Look beyond stereotypes.

Culture is lived, changing and regional.

Language

Hangul is one of the most practical skills a newcomer can learn. Reading basic signs, menus and transport information becomes easier once you recognize the Korean alphabet.

Politeness

Korean has speech levels that communicate social context. In unfamiliar or formal situations, polite language is a safe starting point, while relationships and settings influence how people speak.

Food

Korean food includes rice, soups, noodles, grilled dishes, fermented foods, street food and regional specialties. Famous dishes such as bibimbap, kimchi and bulgogi are only an introduction.

Traditional + modern

Palaces, hanok, crafts and traditional holidays exist alongside K-pop, gaming, design, cafés and contemporary art. Modern Korea continuously reinterprets older culture.

03 • Heritage

Visit with context.

Historic sites tell a story; they are more than backdrops for photos.

Royal palaces and historic districts offer ways to understand Korean history and how older spaces coexist with the contemporary city. When you visit, learn why the place matters and follow its current visitor guidance.

Traditional neighborhoods are also living communities. Respect residents, follow posted rules, and avoid treating residential areas as if they were a theme park.

02 • Deep guide

Seollal — Korean Lunar New Year

Select a festival above. The guide below changes to that festival and stays on this page.

Traditional holiday • Lunar calendar

설날 • Seollal

What is it? Seollal is Korea’s Lunar New Year, a major traditional holiday associated with family gatherings, respect for elders, ancestral remembrance, special foods and hopes for health and prosperity.

Why is it celebrated? It marks the start of a new lunar year and connects ideas of renewal, family, gratitude, respect and good fortune.

Historical/cultural background. Traditional practices include sebae (New Year bowing), tteokguk, family gatherings and traditional games, with regional and family differences in how they are observed.

Current timing

2026 reference

When? Seollal Day in 2026 fell on February 17. Holiday dates follow the lunar calendar and therefore change each year.

What can you do? Visit official cultural programs, palace sites, hanok villages and museums where holiday activities are announced.

Where? In Seoul, 2026 cultural programs included Namsangol Hanok Village, Unhyeongung and major royal heritage sites.

01 • Everyday life

Prepare for ordinary days.

Small practical decisions can have the biggest effect on your first month.

Housing

International students may encounter dormitories, studios, shared homes or private rooms. Compare deposits, rent, utilities, distance to campus, rules and contract conditions before choosing.

Transport

Subways, buses and regional rail are central to life in many Korean cities. Learn your route to campus, understand the local transport-card system and keep a backup route.

Money

Build a real budget around housing, food, transport, phone, insurance, study materials and setup costs. Costs can vary substantially by city and neighborhood.

Connectivity

Plan your phone/eSIM, university email, important addresses and emergency contacts early. Keep your important documents secure in both physical and digital form.

International students can fall under Korea's National Health Insurance system according to the applicable rules. Confirm your current status and contributions through NHIS and your university.

04 • Everyday knowledge

Things newcomers often don't know.

These are the small systems that are easy to underestimate before arrival.

Recycling & waste

Korea has detailed waste-sorting systems and rules that can vary by local government and building. Learn the rules where you live rather than assuming one national household routine applies everywhere.

Convenience stores

Convenience stores are useful for quick meals, drinks, small household supplies, top-ups and everyday services. They are part of daily city life, not only tourist stops.

Delivery culture

Food and parcel delivery are deeply integrated into everyday life. Korean addresses can be structured differently from what newcomers expect, so saving your residence address in Korean can help.

Campus communication

University portals, email, notice boards and student groups can carry different pieces of information. Check your official university channels regularly.
